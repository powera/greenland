## Implementation Notes

### Active vs. Presentational Sentence Dichotomy

As you suggested, a natural dichotomy will emerge between sentences with **active/lexical verbs** vs. sentences with **copular/existential verbs** (helping/absent verbs):

**Active Verb Patterns** (Full lexical verb carries meaning):
- `pronoun_verb_noun` - "He eats bread."
- `noun_verb_noun` - "The cat chases the mouse."
- The verb ("eats", "chases") is the main content word expressing an action

**Copular/Presentational Patterns** (Verb just links or presents):
- `noun_is_adjective` - "The bread is fresh." (copula "is" links subject to description)
- `noun_is_in_place` - "The bread is here." (copula locates the subject)
- `here_is_noun` - "Here is the bread." (presentational/existential - introduces the noun)
- The verb ("is") is grammatically necessary but semantically light/empty

**Key Distinction:**
- **"The bread is here"** - Topic-comment structure (talking ABOUT the bread)
- **"Here is the bread"** - Presentational structure (INTRODUCING the bread's existence/location)
- Both focus on "bread" as the entity, but the verb "is" is just a grammatical helper, not the semantic focus

This is sometimes called:
- **Copular sentences** (be + adjective/noun)
- **Existential sentences** (there is/are)
- **Locative inversion** (Here is / On the table sits...)
- vs. **Dynamic/action sentences** (actual verb-driven events)

### Grouping Strategy

Consider organizing patterns by:
1. **Verb Type**:
   - **Active/Lexical Verbs** (eat, run, chase) - verb carries the meaning
   - **Copular/Light Verbs** (be, seem, become) - verb is grammatical glue
   - **Existential/Presentational** (there is, here is) - introduces entities
2. **Sentence Type**: Statement, Question, Negation
3. **Tense**: Present, Past, Future/Modal
4. **Complexity**: Simple (SV, SVO), Complex (SVOO, prepositional phrases)
5. **Information Structure**:
   - **Topic-Comment** ("The bread is here" - about the bread)
   - **Presentational** ("Here is the bread" - introducing bread's existence)

### Slot Type Extensions

To implement these patterns, you may need to add:
- **verb_present**, **verb_past**, **verb_past_participle** (different verb forms)
- **size_adjective**, **material_noun** (POS subtypes)
- **time_period** (day, week, month, year)
- **noun_plural** (plural forms)

### Pattern Complexity Progression

**Level 1** (Simplest - Currently Implemented - Copular/Light Verbs):
- Basic copula (is/are) with adjectives
- Simple possession (my X is Y)
- Basic location (X is in Y)
- Existential (where is, what is)

**Level 2** (Add next - Introducing Active Verbs):
- Present tense actions (eat, run, see)
- Have/possess constructions
- Basic active verb questions (does he eat?)
- Presentational inversion (here is, there is)

**Level 3** (Medium - Expanding Verb Usage):
- Past tense actions
- Modal verbs (can, should, must)
- Frequency adverbs (always, sometimes)
- Negation with both copular and active verbs

**Level 4** (Advanced - Complex Structures):
- Passive voice (is eaten, was made)
- Comparisons (more/most/as...as)
- Complex questions (why, how)
- Multiple clauses

### Recommended Implementation Order

Start with patterns that:
1. Use high-frequency vocabulary (nouns, verbs everyone knows)
2. Cover essential grammar structures (present tense, questions, negation)
3. Are culturally universal (avoid idioms or culture-specific expressions)
4. Balance copular/light verb patterns with active/lexical verb patterns
