English adverbs have 3 comparative degrees:

**Degrees of Comparison:**
- Positive (base form): quickly, fast, well
- Comparative (comparing two actions): more quickly, faster, better
- Superlative (comparing three or more): most quickly, fastest, best

**Formation Rules:**
- Short adverbs (1 syllable): add -er/-est (fast → faster → fastest, hard → harder → hardest)
- Adverbs ending in -ly: use more/most (quickly → more quickly → most quickly)
- Irregular forms: well → better → best, badly → worse → worst, far → farther/further → farthest/furthest

**Important notes:**
- Many adverbs don't have comparative forms (e.g., "very", "always", "never", "here", "there")
- Some adverbs of manner can take both forms (soon → sooner/more soon → soonest/most soon)
- Use empty string ("") for forms that don't exist or are not applicable
