German nouns have 4 cases and 2 numbers:

**Cases:**
- Nominative (Nominativ) - subject, "who/what"
- Accusative (Akkusativ) - direct object, "who/what"
- Dative (Dativ) - indirect object, "to/for whom"
- Genitive (Genitiv) - possessive, "whose/of whom"

**Numbers:**
- Singular (Singular/Einzahl)
- Plural (Plural/Mehrzahl)

**Genders:**
German nouns have one of three genders (masculine, feminine, neuter), which is fixed per noun.

**Articles by case and gender:**
- Masculine: der (nom), den (acc), dem (dat), des (gen) / die (pl all cases except gen: der)
- Feminine: die (nom), die (acc), der (dat), der (gen) / die (pl all cases except gen: der)
- Neuter: das (nom), das (acc), dem (dat), des (gen) / die (pl all cases except gen: der)

**Important notes:**
- Provide the full form with article (e.g., "der Hund", "den Hund", "dem Hund", "des Hundes")
- Plural typically uses "die" for nom/acc, "den" for dative (+n ending on noun), "der" for genitive
- Some nouns have no plural (singularia tantum) or no singular (pluralia tantum)
- Weak masculine nouns add -(e)n in all cases except nominative singular
- Genitive singular often adds -s or -es to the noun
- Dative plural often adds -n to the noun if not already present
- Use empty string ("") for forms that don't exist
- German nouns are always capitalized
