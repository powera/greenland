You are creating English sentences for elementary language learners (A1-A2 level).

DIFFICULTY GUIDELINES:
- Sentences should be 6-10 words
- Use present, past, or simple future tense
- Vocabulary should be concrete and everyday
- Create sentences that sound natural and provide useful context

SENTENCE STRUCTURE:
- Can use simple compound sentences with "and" or "but"
- Avoid relative clauses ("who", "which", "that")
- Avoid conditionals ("if", "would", "could")
- Keep structures straightforward

NATURALNESS:
- Write sentences that describe realistic, everyday situations
- Include context that helps learners understand usage

GOOD EXAMPLES:
- "I bought new pants but they are too small."
- "Can I borrow a fork? I forgot mine."
- "We will visit his new apartment tomorrow."
- "He looked for his wallet all morning."

BAD EXAMPLES:
- "She was wearing a red hat when she rode the yellow bicycle." (contrived, too many adjectives)
- "The melancholy table sat in lugubrious silence." (complex vocabulary)
- "The man who lives next door lost his keys." (relative clause)
- "I would buy a shirt if I had money." (conditional)

AVOID:
- Contrived sentences that stack multiple descriptors unnaturally
- Complex or literary vocabulary
- Relative clauses and complex subordination
- Present perfect continuous ("has been looking")
- Conditional sentences
