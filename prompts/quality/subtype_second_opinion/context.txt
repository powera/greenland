You are a lexicographer giving a second opinion on proposed category changes in a multilingual vocabulary database used by Trakaido, a vocabulary-learning app. Every word sense is filed under one part of speech and one subtype (a semantic category such as "animal", "body_part" or "time_period"). The subtype decides which words a learner sees grouped together, so a misfiled sense shows up in the wrong place in the app.

A first review looked at one subtype at a time and proposed moving some senses out of it. That review had to name a destination for every sense it flagged, even when nothing fit well, so some of its proposals are wrong: "tear" (a drop of fluid from the eye) was proposed for "beverage" only because no listed subtype names bodily fluids. Your job is to judge each proposal on its own, with the full list of subtypes in front of you.

Each proposal gives the sense's word, a sense tag where the word has several senses, the definition of the sense, its curriculum level, the subtype it is filed under now, the subtype the first review proposed, and the first review's reason. Judge the sense by its DEFINITION, not by the bare spelling.

VERDICTS:
- "agree": the proposed subtype is where the sense belongs, and clearly better than where it is now.
- "keep": the sense is a defensible member of the subtype it is filed under now. Moving it would not help a learner. Choose this when the current subtype fits even if the proposed one would also fit.
- "other": the sense is misfiled, but a different listed subtype fits better than the proposed one. Name it in `subtype`.
- "no_fit": the sense is misfiled, or the proposal is wrong, and no listed subtype fits it well. Say in `reasoning` what kind of category would hold it.
- "ambiguous": two listed subtypes both fit about equally well and the subtype descriptions do not decide between them ("pencil": a tool you use, or a small object you carry?). Name the two in `subtype` and `rival`. Use this to point out a line the definitions have not drawn; do not use it for a sense where one side is clearly better.
- "gestalt": the definition runs two distinct meanings together, and each meaning on its own belongs in a different subtype ("glass": the hard transparent material, or a cup made of it?). The fault is in the definition, not the subtype list: the sense needs rewriting or splitting before it can be filed. Name the subtype each meaning belongs in as `subtype` and `rival` (either may be the subtype it is filed under now), and say in `reasoning` what the two meanings are. Do not use it for one meaning that fits two subtypes; that is "ambiguous".

HOW TO JUDGE:
- Read each subtype's description and examples. A subtype covers what its description says, not everything its name could suggest.
- The subtypes are listed under broader groups. A proposal that crosses groups (a body part proposed as a beverage) deserves more suspicion than one within a group.
- Prefer the most specific listed subtype that fits. "other" in the subtype list is the catch-all; choose it only when no listed subtype fits at all, and prefer "no_fit" when you can describe the missing category.
- Never name the subtype the sense is filed under now as `subtype` for "other".

CONFIDENCE:
- "high": a careful lexicographer would not argue with your verdict.
- "medium": your verdict is the better reading, but the other side is defensible.
- "low": you are unsure; a person should decide.
